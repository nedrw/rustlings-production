# Capacity is a model with numbers in it, not a feeling

## WHY

Every step so far has ended in a measurement: the write path counts its own calls, a segment's bytes
are pinned by a golden vector, pruning is a rate, a run is attributed to the phase that paid, a long
soak watches five live counters. This step is about the question those measurements cannot answer.
*What will a million events a day cost* is asked **before** the load arrives, by somebody deciding
how many machines to buy, and the only honest way to answer it is a model: a handful of numbers
somebody measured once, arithmetic over them, and a list price.

That is a different kind of artifact, and it fails differently. A measurement that is wrong today is
somebody's incident; a model that is wrong keeps being quoted for a year, in a confident voice, on a
page nobody re-runs — and the way it goes wrong is always the same: one constant that was measured
before the encoder under it changed. So this step builds the model and the **comparison** together.
The constants and the store's own numbers are printed by one harness and judged in one lane, and a
constant that stops matching what the store writes turns `slow:bench-budget@10` red under the name
of the quantity it answers for. A capacity model nobody compares with a store is a page; this one is
a design.

The design capability this establishes is `ops/capacity-cost@design`: the numbers an operator
plans with, and the evidence that they are still true.

## TASK

The scaffold created three files and inserted one line:

- `src/engine/cost.rs` — the model, with `todo!`/`panic!` bodies and the whole surface documented;
- `tests/budget_cost.rs` — the harness the lane runs, **complete**: it builds a store through the
  real write path, asks two queries, measures the three file sets and prints both sides. Read its
  module docs before you write anything; every number this step is judged on comes from it;
- `docs/capacity.md` — the page's skeleton, a stub of TODOs;
- and `pub mod cost;` inside the marked block of `src/engine/mod.rs`.

**The model is a value with units in its field names.** `Model` carries one integer per number the
project decided — `bytes_per_sample`, `series_overhead_bytes`, `log_bytes_per_sample`,
`bytes_per_query`, `retention_days`, three prices, and the tolerance the model claims for itself —
and `Model::DEFAULT` is the one this project ships. Each field's documentation says where its number
came from: a measurement of this store (the harness prints its own version of each), a policy
(`lakelet/4.9-retention`'s), or a list price with a date on the page. `Usage` is the question as a
value — a million events a day, a thousand series, ten thousand queries, thirty days — and
`Usage::DEFAULT` is the model's own default question.

**The arithmetic is methods whose names carry the units**, and the money is in **micro-units** of the
dollar: no `f64` anywhere in the cost path, because a cost that depends on how a machine rounds is a
cost two machines disagree about, and the lane compares counts. `sample_bytes_per_day`,
`series_bytes_per_day` and `log_bytes_per_day` are three separate terms; `bytes_per_day` is their
sum; `stored_bytes` is that times the retention; `ingest_micros`, `storage_micros` and
`query_micros` are the three shares of a month and `monthly_micros` is **exactly** their sum,
written as one expression over the three methods so that the identity can be falsified by a case
instead of asserted in prose. `yearly_micros` is `MONTHS_PER_YEAR` of those months; the month is
`DAYS_PER_MONTH` days, named once. Two decisions the documentation argues and the spec pins: a
retention of **zero days is what one day holds** (a running store has a day on the disk before
anything expires — not nothing, and not a division), and the three shares of a total are per-mille
integers that add to exactly a thousand, the largest carrying the rounding.

**Then the half that makes this a design: the comparison.** `Measured` is what a store actually
wrote — segments, their sidecar indexes, the log, records, series, the (segment, series) pairs the
sidecars were written for, and what each query read — and `Model::predicts` is the model's version of
the same numbers. `Model::agrees` puts them side by side: one `Drift` per quantity, each naming the
constant it came out of, the direction (`exact`, `over`, `under`) and the distance in whole per-mille
of what the model said. A model five percent off is inside the tolerance it states; one twenty
percent off is not, and the answer says which constant moved and which way. `Estimate` is the answer
as `name=value` lines in a fixed order, integers only — that is what the page quotes.

**`docs/capacity.md` is the deliverable.** Write it from the model's own output, never by hand: the
block is `Estimate::of(Model::DEFAULT)`, printed line for line, and the retention table is three more
calls to the same model. Then argue each term — where `bytes_per_sample` comes from and what the
harness measured it as, what a series costs the sidecar and which way that term errs at high
cardinality, retention as the only multiplier a person chooses (with the three-row table and what the
ends cost against each other), the query term with `lakelet/4.14-index-pruning`'s rate cited rather
than re-measured, the three prices with their source, their region and **the date the list was read**,
and a section on **what the model is not**: it does not predict latency, it prices no cardinality cap,
it does not price `lakelet/4.8-compaction`'s rewritten bytes or the store's own manifest, and its
query term is one pruned reading rather than an average. Every number on the page is a line of the
block, a cell of the table, or a line of the harness named where it is quoted.

Do not touch `tests/budget_cost.rs` — it is the course's file and it is complete — and do not add a
dependency: `src/engine/cost.rs` is arithmetic over values, and it is the one module in this project
that may not read anything at all.

## TRAP

1. **A float anywhere in the money path.** `14798` micro-units is an integer; `0.014798` is not, and
   twice `0.014798` is not `0.029596` in binary floating point. The moment one term is an `f64`, two
   machines round the same bill differently and the lane — which compares counts — has nothing to
   compare. Every price, every share and every total is an integer, and the division happens once, at
   the end, with `/`.
2. **Measuring inside the model.** The tempting shortcut is to have `Model::predicts` look at the
   store — ask the manifest, stat a directory, read the clock — so that the comparison always
   agrees. That is the failure this whole step is about, one level up: a model that measures is not a
   model, it is a slow measurement, and it cannot answer a question about a store that does not exist
   yet. The spec reads `src/engine/cost.rs` for it: no `std::fs`, no `std::path`, no `std::time`, no
   `std::env`.
3. **A tolerance wide enough to hide the drift.** `agreement_permille` is the one number that can make
   a drifting model look green, and it is stated once, in the model, where the budget lane holds it
   down as an upper bound. Widening it is the same mistake as editing the baseline: the constant did
   not get better, the test did.
4. **A year that is three hundred and sixty-five days.** Compute the year as its own expression over
   the days and it is a different number from twelve monthly bills — by up to eleven micro-units,
   which is exactly the kind of discrepancy that makes two reports of the same year unreconcilable.
   The year is `MONTHS_PER_YEAR` of the months, and the round happens once, in the month.
5. **`todo!()` inside one of the `const fn`s.** The model's arithmetic is `const` — `Usage::DEFAULT`
   is built by one of them — so a `todo!("…")` with a formatted message in one of those bodies is a
   compile error (`E0015`), not a runtime panic. The scaffold's stubs of those functions use
   `panic!("lakelet/4.20-capacity-model: …")` for that reason; write the body, or keep that shape
   while you iterate.
6. **Treating the sidecar's per-series cost as a property of the format.** One index file per
   segment, and its size does not depend on how many series the segment holds — so what a *series*
   costs is a division, and the constant is only right for the workload it was measured on. The
   model errs *high* here at high cardinality, and `docs/capacity.md` has to say so; a reader who
   takes `series_bytes_per_day` as the price of a series will delete series they do not need to
   delete.
7. **Editing the page by hand.** The block and the table are compared line for line and cell for
   cell against calls to the model. A number typed in is a number that stops matching — which is the
   only way a page of numbers stays true, and the reason to write it from a run of the model rather
   than from a run of the harness.
8. **Counting the segments and calling it the store.** This store never truncates its log, so the
   bytes it holds are mostly the raw payloads and not the compressed segments — `log_share_permille`
   is `885` against the samples' `113`. A capacity model that added up segment files would price a
   tenth of the disk, and the drill that finds that is one line of the harness's output.
