# A downsampled answer is still an answer: windows, alignment, tolerance

## WHY

Nine steps and a whole store, and nothing has asked it anything yet. That is what this step is for:
a telemetry store is not a filing cabinet, it is an instrument, and the question everybody asks of
an instrument is *how did this move*. Nobody wants a year of one-second samples — that is thirty-one
million records — so a dashboard wants a point a minute, an alert wants a point an hour, and a
capacity plan wants a point a day. Every one of those is the same question about a **window** of
time, and the interesting part is not the averaging. It is that a downsampled answer can be wrong
while looking completely right, in three ways, and each of them is a decision this step writes down:

- **windows that are not on the data's grid.** Anchor a window at the first record a query happens
  to see and one extra sample at the front shifts every boundary: a dashboard refreshed twice in the
  same hour draws two different step functions for the same hour, and neither of them is wrong. Worse,
  a retention cutoff (`lakelet/4.9-retention` counts in whole buckets) and a query that counts in
  buckets of its own cannot be compared at all. A window is a bucket of the **absolute time axis**,
  `[n * width, (n + 1) * width)` counted from the Unix epoch, and `Window::partition` is
  `Partition`'s own arithmetic rather than a second implementation of it.
- **a gap printed as a zero.** An hour in which nothing was *reported* and a counter that really
  measured zero are different facts — a collector that is down and a machine that is idle — and one
  number claiming to be both makes the difference unreadable for ever. A window with no records in
  the range is **absent** from the answer, and the gap is countable from the windows the range
  touches.
- **a partial bucket that borrowed its neighbours.** A range that begins at 10:30 has half a window
  at one end and probably half a window at the other, and the tempting fix — fill the window from
  whatever is nearby — reads records the caller did not ask about. An answer that reached outside
  the range is an answer to a different question; `Bucket::count` is the honest number that sits
  next to the value, so "mean of twelve samples" and "mean of one" are not the same output.

And the fourth decision is a number in the code. **The mean of the buckets is not the mean of the
range.** They agree exactly only when every window holds records with the same mean; otherwise the
difference is a property of how the range falls across its partial windows, which is not something a
tolerance can be derived from, only *declared*. That declaration is `TOLERANCE` and `agree`, and
every comparison of two computed numbers in this step's spec goes through them — no case compares
two `f64`s with `==`, and none of them would pass if the answer had been computed from the wrong
records either. On this step's own budget workload the two means differ by **0.031 out of 32.4** —
one part in a thousand — and the code declares a twentieth, which is the point: the tolerance is a
statement about wrong records, not a fingerprint of this workload's arithmetic.

The budget lane is where those numbers live, and it is shaped so that the rules above are the ones
being measured. Four segments of thirty minutes, a sample every fifteen seconds, four series, and
**five minutes in the middle in which nothing was reported**; one query about one of the four
series, from thirty seconds into a window to twenty seconds into another:

- **28 windows** are touched, **23 buckets** are emitted, and **5 gaps** are left out — the shape of
  the workload and the "absent, not zero" rule as three counts;
- **400 records** are scanned and **88** are in range: a query's cost before there is an index to
  bring it down (`lakelet/4.14-index-pruning` is that step), and the series filter in between;
- the fullest bucket holds **4 records**, and no bucket is invented, duplicated or counted twice
  (`invented_windows_max` and `records_unaccounted_max` are both zero);
- the two means differ by **0.031** (`mean_gap_max`), which is the tolerance's own metric.

## TASK

Five properties make up this step. Everything else is packaging — including the command line, which
is packaging you have to get exactly right, because a script is what will be running it.

- **A window is a bucket of the absolute time axis.** `Window::new(width_millis)` and
  `Window::partition(ts_millis)` are the whole of the alignment rule: the bucket is
  `floor(ts / width) * width`, half-open, and a timestamp before 1970 falls in the first bucket
  because there is no bucket below the epoch. `Window::partitions(from, to)` is the grid a range sits
  on — every window the half-open `[from, to)` touches, in order, and none for an empty range — and
  it is what makes a gap *countable* rather than invisible. A width of zero is not refused
  *here*: `Partition::of` treats it as the narrowest window there is, because a value type answering
  a hole is worse than a value type answering a small bucket. The parser refuses `--window 0`, where
  a caller's typo is.
- **A bucket counts what is in the range, not what is in the window.** The first and last window of
  an answer are usually partial; they hold the records between the range's edge and the window's
  edge, and nothing else. Nothing fills them from outside the range. `Bucket::count()` is the honest
  number beside `Bucket::value()`, and `Answer::in_range()` is the identity a caller checks the whole
  answer against: the buckets' counts added up are the records the pass says it used.
- **An empty window is absent.** It does not appear in `Answer::buckets()` at all — not with a zero,
  not with a count of zero. A caller who wants to draw the gap has it: the windows
  `Window::partitions` answers, minus the buckets the answer holds. The budget lane's `gaps` counts
  them and `invented_windows_max` refuses to see one printed as a bucket.
- **`series` is an exact name, and `None` is every series.** No prefix, no pattern, no case folding —
  a filter that matched loosely would fold one host's samples into another's and produce a number
  nobody can explain. Both halves are used: `None` when a caller genuinely wants the fleet together,
  and the name when they do not, which is why `lakelet/4.11-query-engine` makes the grammar *ask*
  for a series instead of defaulting to all of them.
- **The tolerance is a number in the code.** `TOLERANCE` (a twentieth) and `agree(left, right)` — a
  relative comparison with a floor of one at the bottom, so that two zeroes are the same answer and
  two values near zero are not compared by a ratio that does not exist. `NaN` agrees with nothing,
  itself included. This is the first declared tolerance in this track;
  `lakelet/4.12-aggregation-correctness` is where the summation error itself becomes the subject,
  and a running sum is compared against a naive one.

**What the course gives you, and what is yours.** The scaffold declares the shape, because the spec,
the harness and the next steps read it:

- `src/engine/query/downsample.rs` — `Window`, `Range`, `Aggregate`, `Bucket`, `Answer`, `TOLERANCE`,
  `agree` and `downsample`, with the doc comments that state the rules above and `todo!()` bodies
  where the rules are implemented. Every body is yours, accessors included: the signatures are the
  interface the spec compiles against, and the semantics are the step;
- `src/engine/query/mod.rs` — the module tree, complete, with a marker block so
  `lakelet/4.11-query-engine` can add `lex`, `parse`, `plan` and `exec` beside `downsample`;
- `src/cli/mod.rs` — `USAGE` (the grammar, byte for byte), `MAX_WINDOWS`, `Outcome` and
  `run(args)` with a `todo!()`: `run` returns what the process would have written and the code it
  would have used, so the contract is checkable without a process and the binary has nothing left to
  decide;
- `src/bin/lakeletq.rs` — the shell, complete: it calls `cli::run` and maps the `Outcome` to two
  streams and an exit code. Nothing in it decides what the answer is;
- `tests/budget_downsample.rs` — the course's harness, which is what `slow:bench-budget@10` runs. It
  is not yours to edit.

**The command line.** `lakeletq` is the read side of this store, and it keeps the contract
`jobq/3.15-cli-contract` established rather than inventing a fifth idea about command lines. Every
line of the table below is checked by running the real binary:

| call | stdout (text) | stdout (`--json`) | code |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lakeletq`, `--help`, `-h`, `help` | the usage text | the usage text | 0 |
| `--version` | `lakeletq <version>` | — | 0 |
| `query --store <dir> --from <ms> --to <ms> --window <dur> --agg <name> [--series <s>]` | one line per bucket | `[{"start":…,"count":…,"value":…},…]` | 0 |
| anything the caller got wrong | *empty* | *empty* | 2 |
| a store directory that is not there | *empty* | *empty* | 3 |
| a store that refused (a manifest or a segment it cannot read) | *empty* | *empty* | 4 |
| an environment that is broken | *empty* | *empty* | 1 |

The pieces of that table that get argued rather than typed:

- **the text shape is the answer and nothing else**: one line per bucket, oldest window first, the
  fields `start count value` separated by single spaces, and a window that held no records is not a
  line. The value prints in its shortest round-trip form, so reading it back gives the same number;
- **`--json` prints one document** with those three fields in that order, `[]` when the range held
  nothing, and the same exit code and diagnosis as the text shape in every case — a format is not a
  second command, and the drift between two code paths is always in the failure handling;
- **`--from`/`--to` are milliseconds since the Unix epoch**, `--window` is a number and a unit
  (`1500ms`, `90s`, `5m`, `1h`, `2d`), and `--agg` is one of `count`, `sum`, `mean`, `min` or `max`.
  A range that ends where it starts — the two flags typed in the wrong order, usually — is refused
  as a caller error, because "no data" is exactly what a script would believe;
- **the store is read, never created.** `Segments::open` creates the directory it is given, which is
  right for the daemon and wrong for a read tool: a typo in `--store` would leave an empty store
  behind and answer `0` with no lines. So `query` checks first — nothing at that path is `3`, and a
  path that is not a directory at all is `1`, because that is the operator's problem rather than the
  script's;
- **the store's floor is an answer, not an error.** A store that has forgotten up to a millisecond
  answers with what it still keeps, so an answer may begin later than `--from` asked.
  `lakelet/4.11-query-engine` is where a *query* refuses a range the store cannot answer;
- **a question has a size.** `MAX_WINDOWS` is how many buckets one query will answer, and the count
  is arithmetic the parser does before a store is opened: `--window 1ms` over a year of range is a
  typo, not a question, and a tool that started allocating for it would be a tool that hangs on a
  typo.

**What you see first.** `cargo test -p lakelet --test spec_downsampling` builds and then fails
sixteen times of seventeen, each failure a `not yet implemented` panic from a `todo!()` in one of the
two files. The case that passes is the last one, `the_query_sources_do_not_reach_into_the_service`:
it reads the sources rather than the answer, and it is in this suite for the tree you end up with
rather than for this one. That is the normal shape: the signatures are the course's, the semantics
are yours, and the harness (`tests/budget_downsample.rs`) is in the same state for the same reason —
the first `todo!()` it reaches is a panic, not a count.

The boundary holds here too: `src/engine/**` may not name the service layer, in code or in prose
(`lakelet/4.1-ingest-protocol` reads those files as text), and a query is engine work — it runs in a
test, in a repair tool and in a load generator that has no configuration file at all. The
command line *is* the one place that is allowed to open a store, and it does it through the same
`Segments::open` everything else uses.

## TRAP

1. **Anchoring the windows at the first record.** It passes every hand-checked example, and it is
   wrong the moment the data moves: the same hour answers two different `start` values, and a
   retention cutoff (a bucket boundary) cannot be compared with a query's buckets at all.
   `Window::partition` is `Partition::of` and nothing else; the spec pins the absolute grid and the
   budget lane's `invented_windows_max` is zero.
2. **Filling the first or last bucket from outside the range.** "It is only half a window" is true
   and irrelevant: those records were not asked about, and the count that comes with them is a count
   of records the caller cannot see. `a_range_that_starts_and_ends_mid_window_leaves_partial_buckets`
   asserts the counts and the identity behind them.
3. **Printing a zero for a window nothing was reported in.** It looks like a nicer graph and it
   destroys the one difference an operator needs — down or idle. The spec asserts the pair, and
   `gaps` in the budget lane is the count of windows the answer is allowed to say nothing about.
4. **Taking `Snapshot::len()` for the answer's size.** `len()` counts what the *segments* hold,
   including the records below the store's floor that `records()` filters out
   (`lakelet/4.9-retention`). An answer that plans on one number and reads another is an answer that
   claims records the store has already forgotten.
5. **A `mean` over mixed series.** The default when `--series` is not given is every series, and a
   mean of two hosts is a number that describes neither. (`lakelet/4.11-query-engine` is where the
   grammar makes the caller say which.)
6. **Accumulating a running mean of means.** `mean` is `sum / count` over one window, computed once,
   from the two numbers the pass kept. Anything that combines partial means re-weights the windows
   by their count — which is the Simpson's-paradox arithmetic the tolerance above exists to bound.
7. **Comparing floats with `==`.** Two sums over the same records in a different order are different
   `f64`s, and a case built on `==` is a flaky test in an afternoon and a mystery in a year. Every
   comparison of two computed numbers here goes through `agree`.
8. **`Segments::open` on a path that is not a store.** It *creates* the directory, so a typo in
   `--store` answers `0` with no lines — the worst kind of wrong answer, because it is
   indistinguishable from a quiet store. `3` and `1` exist so that the two ways a path can be wrong
   are told apart before anything is opened.
9. **`--json` as a second code path.** The JSON branch that "handles" a failure differently drifts
   within a month. The spec runs every failing call twice, with and without `--json`, and compares
   both the code and the diagnosis.
10. **The usage text on stdout after a bad argument.** It is the friendliest thing to print and it is
    what a `$?`-blind pipeline chokes on. Every failure in the spec asserts stdout is *empty*.
11. **Allocating for a question nobody meant.** `--from 0 --to <huge> --window 1ms` is a million
    buckets per millisecond of range. The count is arithmetic, so it is a caller error (`2`) naming
    both numbers, before any store is opened.
12. **Refusing a range the store has partly forgotten.** The store's floor means an answer may begin
    later than `--from`; treating that as an error turns a working store into a broken one for every
    query that reaches back far enough. That refusal belongs to `lakelet/4.11-query-engine`, where it
    can be said in one sentence with a reason.
13. **A `todo!()` left in either file.** `no-todo` runs over every target, and the two files this
    step adds are where the gate will look first.

## FURTHER

- **Time buckets, and the origin they are aligned to.** TimescaleDB's
  [`time_bucket()`](https://docs.tigerdata.com/api/latest/hyperfunctions/time_bucket/) is this step's
  `Window` in SQL: buckets aligned to a *origin* rather than to the first row, an `offset` when the
  alignment has to move, and a documented consequence that the last bucket of a range may hold only
  part of a period. That is the same trade this step makes — the partial bucket at each end — with
  the alternative (align on the data) named as the mistake it is.
- **The other alignment choice, in the wild.** Prometheus's
  [range queries](https://prometheus.io/docs/prometheus/latest/querying/api/#range-queries) take
  `start`, `end` and `step` and place the grid at `start` — so two range queries over the same hour
  with different starts answer different step functions. It is worth reading both to see what the
  choice costs, and then to notice that a *retention* policy cannot be written that way at all: what
  a store forgets has to be a number that does not depend on who asked.
- **Absent versus zero, as an explicit argument.** InfluxDB's Flux
  [`fill()`](https://docs.influxdata.com/flux/v0/stdlib/universe/fill/) makes a caller choose what an
  empty window becomes — nothing, a previous value, or a zero. This step chooses *nothing* and says
  why; the interesting part of that page is how many ways there are to get the choice wrong, and how
  each one is invisible in a graph.
- **Why the mean of the means is not the mean.** The classic statement is
  [Simpson's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson%27s_paradox): the same population can
  reverse a trend when it is grouped differently, which is what a window is. This step's answer is
  not "never aggregate" but "state the tolerance, and carry the count next to the value".
- **Floating point, once, properly.** David Goldberg's
  [*What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic*](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E19957-01/806-3568/ncg_goldberg.html)
  is why `TOLERANCE` is a declaration and not a derivation, and
  `lakelet/4.12-aggregation-correctness` is where the summation error itself — a running sum against
  a naive one — becomes the step.
- **The next readers of all this.** `lakelet/4.11-query-engine` gives the question a grammar
  (`SELECT mean(value) FROM cpu GROUP BY time(5m), series`), a plan and a set of refusals; it reuses
  this module's windows and its tolerance rather than inventing either.
  `lakelet/4.14-index-pruning` is what brings `records_scanned` down from every record in the store
  to the segments a range actually touches — the count this step's budget lane prints as 400.
