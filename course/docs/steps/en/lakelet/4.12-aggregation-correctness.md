# Floats, monotonic time and time zones: the numbers a query is wrong about

## WHY

`lakelet/4.11-query-engine` left you a query engine that parses, prunes, plans and refuses — and
every number it answers with is computed by `+=` over a window. That is the part nobody tests,
because the test everybody writes is the one that passes: four samples of 1, 2, 3 and 4 add up to
10 whatever you do to them.

A store is not four samples. A window is a hundred thousand, the accumulator reaches magnitudes
where one more sample falls below the last place of the total, and every one of those additions
rounds — quietly, in the small ones, in a direction that depends on the order the segments were
read in. The same query over the same records answers differently after a merge. Its mean is a
little wrong, and a capacity plan built on it is wrong by a little in a way nobody can see.

And a day is not twenty-four hours. Every bucket this store has ever computed sits on the absolute
grid counted from the Unix epoch, which is right for a second, a minute and an hour — an hour is an
hour everywhere. A **day** is not: in `Europe/Berlin` one day a year is 23 hours of samples and one
is 25, and a dashboard that counts 86 400-second steps labels two different days with the same date
and drops an hour of the other.

## TASK

Everything you need is in `src/engine/query/agg.rs`, which the scaffold shipped with its
declarations and its rules and no bodies. Four things are decided there, and each of them is a
decision rather than syntax.

**A sum is not `+`.** Write `Sum`: a compensated accumulator that keeps the part of each addition
the rounding threw away and adds it back at the end — Kahan's rule in Neumaier's form. Keep the
count in it too, as a `u64`, because a mean is a sum over a count and a count carried in an `f64`
is the bug a hundred-million-record store finds. `naive` is in the module on purpose: it is the
implementation everybody writes, and this step's cases call it to show what it costs, because a
claim that compensation is necessary cannot be made without running the alternative.

**The bound is a number in the code.** `BOUND` is the error a compensated sum is allowed, relative
to the sum of the magnitudes it added — a scale, not a fingerprint: a sum that cancels has a small
answer and a large budget, and a bound stated against the answer would call it exact. `error_bound`
is that promise as a number a case can ask for, `BOUND * Σ|v|`, and **the magnitudes go through
`Sum` too**: a bound computed by the arithmetic it is bounding would lose the same small terms and
promise less than it means. `agrees` is the comparison for magnitudes; unlike
`lakelet/4.10-downsampling`'s `agree` there is no floor of one underneath it, and `NaN` agrees with
nothing — which is what a broken aggregation produces.

**The answer may not depend on the order the store reads it in.** Segments are read oldest first, a
merge rewrites them, a crash can change an epoch, and a store's data is full of clocks that stepped
backwards. So `lakelet/4.10-downsampling`'s window pass accumulates through `Sum` rather than
through `+=`, and a record is placed in its bucket by its timestamp and never by where it sat in
the read. That is one edit inside `downsample.rs` and it is not optional: it is the difference
between an answer that is a function of the records and one that is a function of the read.

**A day is not twenty-four hours.** `Zone` is a name and the offset transitions **this build
ships**: `UTC`, `Europe/Berlin` with its two years of transitions, and a fixed `±HH:MM` offset that
is arithmetic rather than a table, because a fixed offset never goes stale and a table always does.

- `Zone::parse` answers the names in `Zone::SHIPPED` and a `±HH:MM` offset with exactly two digits
  in each field, and refuses everything else as a **caller error** (`Refusal::Syntax`, class `2`)
  rather than defaulting to UTC: a `--zone Europe/Berlinn` that quietly answered UTC is a dashboard
  a day out that nobody can explain.
- `Zone::offset_at` answers `None` **outside the table** rather than guessing. This build knows
  2024 and 2025; it does not know 2019, and inventing `+01:00` for it would be a confident answer
  to a question there is no data for. `Zone::covers` is the span, and the refusal names it.
- `Zone::local_day` is the half-open span of the *local* day an instant falls in — 23 hours on the
  day the zone springs forward, 25 on the day it falls back. Resolve local midnight against the
  offset in force **at that midnight**; the offset at your own instant is the wrong one on the day
  a zone changes.

**The command line.** `--zone <NAME|±HH:MM>` (default `UTC`) is a flag beside `--store` rather than
part of the query text, because `GROUP BY time(1d)` says how wide a bucket is and not which
midnight it starts at. The rule is one window wide and narrow on purpose:

- with a window of **exactly one day**, the buckets are **local days** of that zone, so their
  boundaries move and their counts change — a spring-forward day holds 23 hours of samples where
  the grid holds 24, and the first and last day of a range are partial;
- with **any other window** the zone changes nothing at all, because an hour is an hour everywhere
  and a rule that moved a five-minute bucket would be a second grid beside the one
  `lakelet/4.9-retention` counts in and `lakelet/4.14-index-pruning` will prune against;
- a range the named table does not cover is refused as a **caller error** naming the covered span,
  not answered approximately.

`UTC` is the default and is not a special case anywhere: UTC's local days *are* the absolute grid,
so a caller who says `--zone UTC` and a caller who says nothing get the same bytes. That is the
invariant to keep while you wire it up.

**What the course gives you, and what is yours.**

- `src/engine/query/agg.rs` — **yours**. The scaffold wrote the declarations, the doc comments and
  `todo!()` bodies; every body is yours, accessors included. The signatures are the interface and
  the rules are the work.
- `src/engine/query/mod.rs` — the module tree, with `pub mod agg;` already added inside its marker
  block.
- `src/engine/query/downsample.rs` — **yours**, and it needs two things: its window `Tally`
  accumulating through `Sum` instead of `+=`, and a way to answer a question on a zone's local days
  when the window is a day. `Answer`, `Bucket` and `downsample`'s own signature do not change —
  `lakelet/4.10-downsampling`'s seventeen cases are still a promise you are keeping.
- `src/engine/query/exec.rs` and `src/cli/mod.rs` — **yours**. The executor needs an entry point
  that takes a zone (keep `execute`'s signature: a bare `execute` is what "no zone" means), and the
  tool needs the flag in **both** input forms, because a second input form may not answer the same
  question differently.
- `src/cli/mod.rs`'s `USAGE` — **yours, and the scaffold could not do it for you.** `USAGE` is one
  `const &str`, so a scaffold (which may only create files or insert lines inside a marker block)
  cannot rewrite it; the `--zone` half of the usage text is your edit. The spec pins it with
  `USAGE.contains("--zone")` and `--help` is compared with the constant itself, so a usage text
  that does not name the flag fails the case rather than the reader.
- `EXPLAIN` gains **no key**. `lakelet/4.11-query-engine`'s spec counts the plan's lines, and a
  thirteenth would break a step you already passed. The zone is visible where it belongs: in the
  bucket boundaries and the bucket counts.

**What you see first.** `cargo test -p lakelet --test spec_aggregation` builds and then fails
sixteen times out of seventeen — each failure a `not yet implemented` panic from a `todo!()`, plus
the usage text, which does not name a flag yet. That is the normal shape.

## TRAP

1. **The correction that swallows the sum.** A Neumaier step that computes `lost` unconditionally
   writes `inf - inf` for a workload containing an infinity and answers `NaN` for a sum that is
   perfectly well defined: `1.0 + inf` is `inf`, and `running(&[1.0, f64::INFINITY])` must be
   `inf`. Skip the correction when the addition did not round *for a reason an `f64` can round* —
   an infinity, a `NaN`, or an overflow.
2. **A bound computed by the arithmetic it bounds.** `error_bound` written as `BOUND * naive(...)`
   over the magnitudes loses the small terms exactly the way the sum did, and promises a bound
   orders of magnitude below what it means. The case is the one that catches it:
   `error_bound(&values) > BOUND * 1e16` for a workload whose magnitudes hold a hundred thousand
   ones.
3. **A local day found from the offset at the caller's instant.** On 2024-10-27 in
   `Europe/Berlin`, an instant at 12:00 local is on `+02:00` and the local midnight that opens that
   day is on `+02:00` — but an instant at 23:30 local is on `+01:00` and the same midnight is
   *still* on `+02:00`. `local_day` resolves the midnight against the offset in force at the
   midnight, and the case that catches a one-pass version asserts the span either side of the
   transition: `(1_729_980_000_000, 1_730_070_000_000)`, twenty-five hours.
4. **`--zone` threaded into one input form and not the other.** The two forms are one question
   asked two ways, and this is the flag where they can drift: `SELECT count(value) FROM cpu WHERE
   ts BETWEEN a AND b GROUP BY time(1d)` with `--zone Europe/Berlin` and the flag form with the
   same range and window must print the same bytes. Keep `--zone` beside `--store` — a flag in
   both — and keep `--from`/`--to`/`--window`/`--agg`/`--series` refused beside `--sql`.
5. **Answering a range the table does not cover.** `Europe/Berlin` in this build is 2024 and 2025.
   A range in 2023 is not an answer with fewer buckets and it is not a `None` a caller has to
   notice: it is a refusal, class `2`, in a sentence that names the span the table covers. The same
   goes for a record outside the span once you let a pass see one — the refusal comes before the
   pass, not inside it.
6. **A count carried in an `f64`.** `Bucket::count` and `Sum::count` are `u64`, and the *mean* is
   the only thing a division is allowed to round. `sum.mean()` over a hundred thousand values of
   `0..99_999` is exactly `49_999.5`; a count that went through a float would answer something
   plausible instead.

## FURTHER

- `src/engine/query/downsample.rs` — `TOLERANCE` and `agree`: this step's own tolerance, and the
  reasoning behind a *declared* bound instead of a floating-point epsilon.

  grid is made of, and the half-open `[start, end)` a local day has to match.

  pick whichever addend is larger.

  and its own documented limits.

  command that prints the transitions this build's table was transcribed from.

  path leaves a live mutant behind.
