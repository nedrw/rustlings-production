# Retry with a budget: jitter, caps, and a breaker

## WHY

`relay/2.6-at-least-once` decided that a failed attempt leaves the delivery owed, and wrote down a
constant: five attempts, then stop. That constant is where the policy should be. Between the two
sits everything that separates a service that recovers from one that makes its own outage worse.

Three failures of that policy are worth naming, because each one is a page of an incident report.

**A subscriber that comes back is hit by everything at once.** Every delivery that failed while it
was away retries on the same schedule, so the fleet wakes up in the same millisecond and the
subscriber that just finished starting gets the whole backlog as a burst. This is the thundering
herd, and the fix is not "wait longer" — the aggregate load is roughly right — it is to *spread*
the retries out: the same total, arriving at different instants.

**A subscriber that is gone eats the queue.** Retrying each delivery five times is fine against an
endpoint that is mostly up. Against one that is down, every delivery in the queue spends its whole
budget: a turn claims sixteen rows, sends sixteen requests into a wall that will not answer them,
and the deliveries behind them wait for turns that produce nothing. The work is not the retry; the
work is learning that the endpoint is down, once, and stopping.

**A retry policy nobody can describe is a retry policy nobody can debug.** "Somewhere between
thirty seconds and an hour, I think" is what an operator gets from jitter drawn out of a random
number generator. The delivery's next attempt instant is on the row, so it had better be a function
the worker can compute twice and get the same answer.

So this step is the policy: an exponential wait, spread deterministically, capped, with a bounded
number of attempts, and a breaker that stops talking to an endpoint before the queue is spent on it.
It is a `design` step, so it also delivers the record that argues the numbers — a budget is a set of
trade-offs, and a trade-off with no written argument gets "fixed" by the next person who sees a
slow retry in a log.

### `src/backoff.rs`

- **`Budget::delay(delivery, attempt)`** — the wait before the attempt that follows `attempt`
failures. The window is `base` doubled once per failure, capped at `cap`; the delivery's place
*inside* the window comes from a hash of `(delivery id, attempt)`.
- **Where in the window, and why not the whole window.** The textbook answer is *full jitter*: a
uniform draw over `[0, window]`. It decorrelates exactly as well as this does, and it has a wait of
almost zero at the bottom — a retry that lands in the same millisecond as the failure it is
retrying, which is a spin wearing a backoff's clothes. It also breaks the step before this one:
`spec/2.6` asks a worker for a second turn *immediately* after a failure and expects the delivery to
be waiting, so the wait must have a floor. Spread over the **upper half** of the window
(`[base/2, base]`), and say in the record why full jitter lost.
- **A hash, not a generator.** `Uuid::as_u64_pair()` gives you 128 bits of id; mix them with the
attempt number through a splitmix64-style finalizer and take the top 53 bits as an `f64` in
`[0, 1)`. No `rand`, no state, and the same wait on every worker and every rerun. Neighbouring
ids have to land far apart in the window — that is what "decorrelate" means — so mix, do not
`% 100`.
- **The doubling has to stop.** `1u32 << 40` panics in a debug build and a wait that wraps around
is a wait of milliseconds. Clamp the number of doublings, use `saturating_mul`, and `.min(cap)`.
- **`Budget::gives_up(attempt)`** — the attempt budget, `relay/2.6`'s `MAX_ATTEMPTS` with a name.
The default budget keeps 5 attempts, a 30-second base and a 6-hour cap: those are given, and what
this step adds is the shape.
- **The breaker.** `BreakerPolicy { failures, cooldown }` per *endpoint*, with `Admission` as the
answer: `Try`, `Probe` while half open, or `Shed`. Count **consecutive retryable failures**, reset
them on a success, and treat what happens after the cooldown as a probe: exactly one attempt goes
out, and its answer decides whether the breaker closes (a success) or opens again for a fresh
cooldown (a failure). A probe that is never reported — the worker died holding it — must be
presumed lost after another cooldown, or one dead process leaves an endpoint cut off for good.
- **Time is a parameter, not an ambient read.** `delay` takes the delivery, `admit` takes the
instant. The alternative — `tokio::time::pause()` with `#[tokio::test(start_paused = true)]`, which
`mailer/1.14-worker` uses for its worker loop — is the wrong tool here on three counts: `test-util`
is not a feature of `tokio` in this build graph, a scaffold may not rewrite the `tokio` line in
`Cargo.toml`, and a *policy* that reads the clock is a policy that cannot be judged without one.
The clock belongs to the worker loop (`relay/2.9-ordering` and `relay/2.15-shutdown-leases`); the
policy takes what it is told.
- **`Clone` shares the breaker.** The state lives behind an `Arc<Mutex<…>>`: a worker hands the same
budget to every turn it runs, so the endpoint stays cut off across turns. A breaker rebuilt per
turn is a breaker that protects one turn.

### `src/delivery.rs`

- **`tick_with_budget(pool, sender, worker, budget)`** — the turn with the policy it runs with, and
the seam this step adds: `pub async fn tick_with_budget(pool: &PgPool, sender: Arc<dyn Sender>,
worker: &str, budget: &Budget) -> Result<Tick, Error>`. The sender is an `Arc` because a turn is
allowed to own what it runs concurrently (that is `relay/2.9-ordering`); the budget is the policy.
`tick` — the signature `relay/2.6` pinned — stays, keeps the default policy, and stays a one-shot
turn.
- **Wire the budget in.** `mark_retry` writes `now + budget.delay(delivery, attempt)`, and the last
attempt is `budget.gives_up(attempt)` rather than a constant. Move the constants out of this file
rather than leaving a second copy next to the policy.
- **Ask the breaker before the attempt, and record its answer.** A shed delivery is **not
attempted**: hand the row back to the queue with `claim::release` and count it in `Tick::shed`.
Its `attempts` does not move and its `next_attempt_at` does not move — that is the promise a
breaker makes, and a shed row left `claimed` is a delivery nobody looks at until its lease expires.
- **A permanent failure is not the endpoint's health.** A `410 Gone` tells you about one delivery;
the breaker counts the failures a retry would have hit again. Say that in the record: it is why a
poison endpoint's deliveries still die one by one instead of being shed together (`relay/2.10-dlq`
is where they land).
- **The default breaker may not open before one delivery has spent its own attempts.** `failures`
must be at least `max_attempts`, because `spec/2.6` counts *turns*: a breaker that opens while a
delivery still has attempts left sheds the turns that would have buried it, and the step before
this one goes red. The default (5 and 5) is that constraint made a number.

The decision record, in the project next to the code it is about, with the three sections the

## TRAP

1. **Full jitter, taken literally.** A uniform draw over `[0, window]` answers a wait of almost
   nothing often enough to matter, the retry fires in the same millisecond as the failure, and
   `spec/2.6`'s "asked again when the retry is due, not before" fails on the final tree. Spread over
   the upper half, or any window with a floor.
2. **Jitter from `rand`.** It decorrelates and it destroys reproducibility: two workers compute
different waits for one delivery, a log line that says when the next attempt is cannot be checked,
and a test that wants to assert a wait has to accept a range. A hash of `(id, attempt)` is stable
across processes and reruns, which is the property this step is paying for.
3. **`1u32 << attempt`.** Four hundred doublings is a panic in debug and a wrap-around in release,
and a wait that wraps is a wait of milliseconds on a delivery that has failed forty times. Clamp
the doublings, `saturating_mul`, then `.min(cap)`.
4. **Jitter that is constant.** `attempt * base + (id % 2)` decorrelates nothing, and neither does
any expression whose range is a rounding error next to the window. Sixty deliveries that failed
together have to land in sixty different places; the spec checks that they do.
5. **Shedding without releasing.** The row was claimed, the attempt is skipped, and the worker moves
on: the delivery is `claimed` until its lease expires (sixty seconds of invisibility) and the turn
after that claims it again, sheds it again, and writes it back. Release it, and count it.
6. **A breaker that charges for the attempt it refused.** If a shed delivery still goes through
`mark_retry`, its `attempts` grows and its budget is gone by the time the endpoint recovers — the
endpoint was down, and the delivery was punished for it. The spec's last test is exactly this.
7. **A breaker that opens on the first failure, or on any failure.** One hiccup is not an outage,
and a permanent `410` is not evidence about the endpoint: count *consecutive retryable* failures,
and reset them on a success.
8. **A half-open breaker with no way back.** One probe goes out, the worker holding it dies, and the
endpoint is cut off forever because nobody ever reports the probe. Give the probe a window of its
own and treat it as lost when it runs out.
9. **A breaker in a `static`.** The process-wide shortcut makes the breaker survive turns, and it
also makes it survive *tests*: a spec that breaks an endpoint leaves it broken for every test in the
same binary, and the failure looks like a flaky policy rather than shared state. The budget is the
thing that is shared, and it is passed in.
10. **A second copy of the constants.** Leaving `MAX_ATTEMPTS` (or a `BACKOFF` next to it) in
`delivery.rs` while the policy answers the same question is how two limits drift apart: the row
says one thing, the worker does another, and the difference shows up as "attempts" that never quite
reaches the number the operator was told.
11. **A default budget that is a different policy.** The defaults are values, and values nobody
argued are values nobody can defend. 5 attempts, 30 seconds, 6 hours and a 5-failure breaker are
`relay/2.6`'s constants given a shape — if you change them, the record has to change with them, and
`spec/2.6` has to still pass.
