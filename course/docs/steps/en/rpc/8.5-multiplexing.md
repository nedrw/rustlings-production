# One connection, many streams, no head-of-line blocking

## WHY

HTTP/2 multiplexes: one connection carries every call, each stream with its own flow-control
window. Multiplexing exists to remove head-of-line blocking — a slow consumer on one stream must not
stop the others — and that only works if the windows are consulted **per stream**. A single
connection-wide queue reintroduces exactly the blocking HTTP/2 was invented to remove, one layer
higher and harder to find: the fast call is not blocked by the network, it is blocked by the
scheduler.

The pump is the scheduler, and its fairness rule is the design decision: one item per stream per
round, repeating until a full round sends nothing. That keeps a big stream from starving a small
one, and it makes a blocked stream a *skipped* stream rather than a stopped pump.

## TASK

The scaffold added `src/multiplex.rs` and its `pub mod multiplex;` line.
[`Connection`] and its accessors are given; `pump` is the stub.

- **[`pump`]** — round-robin passes over the streams, one item each per round, skipping a stream
  whose window is full; stop when a whole round sends nothing; answer the `(index, item)` pairs in
  the order they were sent.

## DONE WHEN

- `kiln run rpc/8.5-multiplexing` — the spec passes
- `kiln check rpc/8.5-multiplexing` — every must gate is green
- a blocked stream at index 0 does not stop the stream at index 1, and a window of two each sends
  two rounds in an interleaved order

## TRAP

1. **A connection-wide buffer.** Collect everything and send it in order: one slow consumer delays
   every other stream — the head-of-line blocking this step is about.
2. **Stopping the round at the first `None`.** A blocked stream ends the pass and the streams after
   it never send; the spec's *"a blocked stream does not stop the others"* is exactly this.
3. **One stream at a time.** Draining stream 0 before stream 1 is fair only if stream 0 is small;
   the round-robin order is what keeps the answer bounded.
4. **Not stopping when nobody sends.** A `loop` without the "no progress" exit spins forever on a
   connection where every window is full.
