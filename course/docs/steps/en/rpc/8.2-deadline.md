# A deadline is absolute, and every hop pays from it

## WHY

A timeout is a duration, and a duration starts when it is read. A deadline is a point in time, and a
point in time is the same number on every hop: the client computes `now + budget`, the server reads
that instant, subtracts what it spent, and passes the rest on. That is why gRPC sends a deadline on
the wire rather than a timeout — with a duration, a three-hop chain has three clocks and its real
budget is the sum.

The boundary is the lesson: a hop may consume **exactly** what is left (the call then has zero
budget and is finished), and a hop that needs more fails **before it runs**. A server that starts
work it cannot finish spends the next hop's budget on a result nobody will read, and the client
receives `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` anyway — later, and with the work wasted.

## TRAP

1. **`>=` at the hop.** "A hop that needs everything that is left fails" turns every call that
   finishes exactly on budget into a timeout, and the last hop of a well-tuned chain is always the
   one that fits exactly.
2. **A duration per hop.** Resetting the clock at each hop is the timeout model, and the chain is
   as long as its hop count; the spec's arithmetic has one deadline for the whole walk.
3. **Failing after the work.** Checking the deadline after the hop runs spends the budget and then
   reports the failure — the wasted-work case the rule exists to prevent.
4. **Forgetting the start.** `call(deadline, now, …)` with `now >= deadline` must fail even when
   `hops` is empty: the client is already out of time before it asks.
