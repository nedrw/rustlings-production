# The term, the vote, and the heartbeat: a small Raft that three nodes agree on

## WHY

`5.2` wrote down four rules. This step makes them *move*: three processes, one log, and a rule
about who may decide what. Everything a replicated key-value store does later — the reads that must
not be stale, the membership changes, the partition drills — is built on the answer to one question:
**how do three processes that cannot see each other agree that a write happened?**

The answer this step implements is Raft's, and it is worth implementing rather than importing
(`openraft` is named in FURTHER): a protocol you did not write is a protocol you cannot test. The
spec below can say "a node grants a vote only to a candidate whose log is at least as long as its
own" and *mean* something about your code, because the code is yours.

Two shapes make that possible, and both are the point of the step:

- **The state machine is a value.** `src/raft/state.rs` has no socket, no clock and no randomness in
  it: a node's state changes because a message arrived or because somebody proposed a write. The
  plumbing that moves bytes and waits for timeouts lives in the *harness* the step ships
  (`tests/sim_raft.rs`), and the two never meet. That is not tidiness — it is what makes a protocol
  testable at all.
- **The schedule is a seed.** The harness runs the cluster inside `turmoil`, a simulator with a
  virtual clock and a virtual network. The seed decides the order the messages arrive in, so
  "the same run again" is a thing the machine can promise, and "a different interleaving" is a thing
  the gate can ask for.

## TASK

- `src/raft/message.rs` — the four messages (`RequestVote`, `Vote`, `Append`, `AppendAck`) and their
  text encoding. Parsing is strict: a line that is not one of the four is a refusal that quotes the
  line, and an entry's payload rides along as hex so that a write containing a newline cannot break
  the line it is in.
- `src/raft/state.rs` — the state machine:
  - `campaign()`: become a candidate in the next term, vote for yourself, ask everyone;
  - a vote is granted only when the candidate's log is at least as long as yours **and** nobody else
    has your vote in this term;
  - a majority of granted votes makes you the leader;
  - a leader appends its own writes to its log and sends them, and a follower that accepts them
    answers how far its log now reaches;
  - a leader commits the largest position a **majority** holds — and only a position whose entry is
    of the leader's **own term**;
  - a message from a newer term makes you a follower again; one from an older term is refused with
    your own term.
- `src/raft/state.rs`, `become_leader`: a new leader writes **one empty entry of its own term** before
  it replicates anything. The spec checks it is there, and the reason is in the TRAP.

The harness is course content and is already in your tree: read `tests/sim_raft.rs` to see what your
state machine is asked to do, and where the election timeout and the heartbeat live.

## DONE WHEN

```sh
cargo test -p meshkv --test spec_teaching_raft          # the rules, one case at a time
KILN_SIM_SEED=1 cargo test -p meshkv --test sim_raft -- --nocapture
```

The second one prints a transcript like

```text
meshkv-sim leader=n3
meshkv-sim committed=1
meshkv-sim entries=2
```

and the same seed prints the same three lines, while another seed elects another node.

## TRAP

- **The candidate's own vote counts.** A candidate in a three-node cluster needs one more vote, not
  two. (The spec is written so that a leader which counts itself twice commits with fewer than a
  majority — a bug this step's own instrument caught while it was being written.)
- **Only an entry of the leader's own term commits.** A leader that inherited entries from an older
  term cannot commit them by itself, which is why it writes an empty entry of its own term when it
  wins: the empty entry commits, and the inherited ones come with it. A leader that skips it can
  hold a majority of an old entry for ever and never commit it.
- **"My log is at least as long as yours" compares the term first.** A log with more entries from
  an older term is *behind* one with fewer entries from a newer term. Comparing the lengths instead
  of the positions elects a leader whose log gets overwritten.
- **A refusal is an answer.** A node that grants a vote to nobody says so; it does not stay silent.
  Silence is how a candidate waits for ever.
- **The follower's log is corrected, never merged.** The first entry the leader and the follower
  disagree on decides everything after it: the follower's suffix is dropped and the leader's is
  written. A committed entry never disagrees — that is the whole safety argument, and the spec
  checks the direction of the correction.
- **The payload is opaque to the consensus layer.** A replicated write is bytes the log carries and
  nothing more; what those bytes *mean* is `5.10`'s subject, and a protocol that starts reading
  them has taken on a job it cannot undo.

## FURTHER

- Raft's paper (§5.1–§5.4) is the source of every rule above; §5.4.2 ("Leader completeness") and
  Figure 8 are the two paragraphs behind the current-term clause and the empty entry.
- `openraft` is the production shape of what you just wrote: the same four ideas, the same types
  (`Term`, `LogId`, `Quorum`), a great deal more machinery. Reading its `RaftLog` after this step
  is a good way to see which parts of Raft are the protocol and which are the engineering.
- `5.13` replaces the harness's virtual network with a richer one (`madsim`), and `5.14` uses it to
  cut the cluster in half — the point of a deterministic simulator is that the partition becomes
  an *input* rather than a thing you wait for.
