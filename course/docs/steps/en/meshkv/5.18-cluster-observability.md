# Who is behind, who is flapping: the SLO page of a cluster

## RECALL

- `obs/metrics-slo@aware` — `mailer/1.20`: the golden signals, `/metrics`, and naming a metric so
  somebody else can find it.
- `obs/metrics-slo@apply` — `relay/2.18`: latency quantiles and backlog per tenant, read off counters.
- `obs/metrics-slo@design` — `jobq/3.17`: error budgets, the alert thresholds that spend them, and the
  judgement without metrics when there are none.

## DELTA

Three services in, the signals are familiar and the *failure* is not. A service fails by being slow or
wrong; a cluster fails by disagreeing, and the counters that show it are ones a service never has:

- **behind**: a follower whose applied index falls further and further behind the leader's commit is
  the node that will be last to notice a leader change and the first to be a problem when one happens.
- **flapping**: a node that stands for election over and over while another keeps winning has a clock
  or a network problem; a node that refuses writes while committing none was on the wrong side of a
  partition.

The SLO is the same idea as `jobq/3.17` applied to *agreement*: an objective is a promise about a
ratio, and the error budget is what is left of it. What is new is that the budget here is arithmetic
over counters — integers, not times — because a budget that needs a clock is a budget that moves when
the machine does. This step does not re-derive the budget; it puts it on a cluster.

## TASK

- `src/metrics.rs` — the module this step adds:
  - `NodeMetrics`: the peer indexes, the elections stood and won, the refusals and commits, the
    applied index;
  - `lag(peer, commit)`: an unknown peer has an *unknown* lag, not a zero one — the refusal says so;
  - `most_behind(commit)`: the peer an operator should look at first;
  - `lost_elections` and `is_suspect`: the two shapes a failing node has;
  - `Slo::new(objective_ppm)` and `budget_left(commits, attempts)`: the ratio arithmetic, in integers.
- `docs/slo.md` and `tests/budget_slo.rs` are course content: the page (`links`) and the harness the
  `bench-budget` gate reads.

## DONE WHEN

```sh
cargo test -p meshkv --test spec_cluster_observability     # the five cases
cargo test -p meshkv --test budget_slo -- --nocapture
```

The second prints the lags, the flapping counters and the remaining budget: ten and fifty entries
behind, no lost elections, eight failures of budget left out of ten.

## TRAP

- **An unknown lag is not zero.** A peer nobody has heard from has no lag to report; a counter that
  answered zero would make the quietest node look like the healthiest one.
- **A peer ahead of the commit is not "negative behind".** The lag floors at zero.
- **Losing elections is a signal, and winning them is not enough.** A node that stood nine times and
  won once is the node to look at, even though it won.
- **Refusals without commits is the other shape.** A node that refused writes and committed none was
  on the wrong side of a partition — the counters say which node that was.
- **The budget is integer arithmetic.** `attempts × (1 − objective) − failures`, floored at zero; a
  budget computed in floating point is a budget that differs by one on another machine, and the one it
  differs by is the one somebody's release decision hangs on.

## FURTHER

- `5.22`'s audit is the other half: the counters here are what each node says about *itself*, and the
  audit compares the nodes' views against each other.
- The SLO's shape is `lakelet/4.20`'s capacity model applied to availability rather than bytes: a
  prediction, a measurement, and the drift between them as the thing that gets judged.
