# A replay is a range, not a position

## WHY

Sooner or later somebody needs yesterday's events again: a downstream table was wrong, a projection
is being rebuilt, an auditor wants the range. None of that is *consumption* — the group's committed
offset is a promise about what has been processed by the service, and a replay must not move it, or
the live consumer starts again from wherever the auditor stopped.

The shape that keeps the two apart is a replay object over a half-open range: it reads `from`
onwards, stops before `to`, and holds no commit. `to` exclusive is the same convention as every
range in this course — `[from, to)` — and it is what makes "replay the broken window" expressible
without an off-by-one.

## TASK

The scaffold added `src/replay.rs` and its `pub mod replay;` line. `new` (which clamps `to` to the
log's end) and `remaining` are given; `next_record` is the stub.

- **[`Replay::next_record`]** — the record at the replay's next offset, advancing; `None` once the
  range is walked out or the log ends inside it.
- **A replay never touches a consumer.** The spec polls and commits a live consumer around a replay
  and expects its position unchanged.

## DONE WHEN

- `kiln run broker/7.5-replay` — the spec passes
- `kiln check broker/7.5-replay` — every must gate is green
- a replay of `[1, 3)` over three records yields the second and third, and the live consumer still
  reads the second next

## TRAP

1. **Committing from a replay.** The natural "it is a consumer, just older" thought: the live group
   then resumes at the auditor's position. The spec's *"the replay committed something"* fails.
2. **An inclusive `to`.** `[1, 3)` reading 1, 2, 3 re-processes one record twice across two
   adjacent windows — the arithmetic every range bug has.
3. **Starting at `from - 1`.** The off-by-one in the other direction: the first record of every
   replay is applied twice, and an idempotent effect hides it until it does not.
4. **Letting the range run past the log.** `next_record` on a `to` beyond the end must answer `None`
   rather than panic; the clamp is in `new`, and the read's `Option` is the second line of defence.
