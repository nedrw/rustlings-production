# Commit after the work, and a restart resumes there

## WHY

The committed offset is the only thing a consumer group leaves behind for its next life. Everything
about restart behaviour follows from one convention and one habit:

- **the commit is the next offset to read**, not the last one read. `commit(1)` after processing the
  record at offset `0` means "start again at 1". Committing the offset you just read re-delivers that
  record forever, which is the off-by-one every consumer starts with;
- **commit after the work**, not before. A commit that runs first is a promise the process has not
  kept: crash after the commit and the record is never processed. Crash after the work and before
  the commit, and the record is delivered again — which is what *at-least-once* means, and why the
  work has to be idempotent.

The log is the truth about what exists, so a commit cannot claim more than the log holds: an offset
past the end is clamped, and a partition the topic does not have is not a position at all.

## TASK

The scaffold added `src/consumer.rs` and its `pub mod consumer;` line. `poll`, `resume`, `committed`
and the constructor are given; `commit` is the stub.

- **[`Consumer::commit`]** — record the group's position for a partition:
  - a partition the topic does not have: ignore it;
  - an offset past `topic.end(partition)`: clamp to the end;
  - anything else: record it as given.
- **A restart resumes at the commit** — `Consumer::resume` is given, and the spec's "at-least-once"
  test reads the uncommitted case: the records are delivered again.

## TRAP

1. **Committing the offset you read.** `commit(&topic, 0, 0)` after reading offset `0` resumes at
   `0` and the record is delivered forever; the spec's *"resumes where it stopped"* fails.
2. **Committing before the work.** The spec cannot see the order of the two statements, but the
   uncommitted test is the same arithmetic from the other side: progress without a commit is
   re-delivered, and that is the price that makes at-least-once honest.
3. **Recording a commit past the end.** A restart reads nothing (fine) and a lag calculation in a
   later step goes negative — the spec clamps at the end.
4. **A commit for a partition that does not exist.** It looks harmless and it hides a caller that is
   talking about the wrong topic; the spec's "ignored" test keeps the map honest.
