# Tracing a queue: propagation into workers, and sampling that keeps the tail

## RECALL

- `obs/tracing-propagation@apply` — `relay/2.19-trace-propagation`: **the ids are written down, not
  passed along.** A delivery's trace was stored on the row the work happens on, every hop got a span
  of its own, the header was parsed and regenerated rather than forwarded, and a header the service
  could not read started a trace instead of refusing the request. That is the same shape here, and
  the queue makes every sentence of it harder: a delivery service's worker is *its* process, and this
  queue's worker may be a different binary, on another host, started by another deployment.
- `obs/metrics-slo@design` — `jobq/3.17-error-budget-alerts`: **the sample and the line.** This step
  grows the line: `Attempt` already writes the fields a query needs about one attempt, and the trace
  fields are the last four — the trace id, this hop's span, and whether the hop is kept. The two
  steps are one story: the counters say a queue is burning, the lines say which jobs and why, and
  the trace is what turns "which jobs" into "which request, from which caller".
- `delivery/dlq@operate` — `jobq/3.16-dlq-tooling`: **a second life does not start a new trace.** A
  replay writes a job's row back into the queue, and the row is where the trace lives — so the ids
  travel through the replay untouched. That is not an accident of this step; it is the reason an
  incident that ends in a replay is one trace from the request that submitted the job to the attempt
  that finally worked.

## DELTA

**The context has to cross a boundary that has no caller on the other side.** `relay/2.19` carried a
`traceparent` from an HTTP request into a delivery; the request was *there*, in its extensions and in
its span, while everything that admitted it was running. Here the queue is the boundary: the request
that accepted the job has returned, the worker that runs it may be a replica the request never
spoke to, and a context kept in a header map, a task-local or a span is gone by then. The row is the
only carrier — which is also why the ids are columns and not a middleware's business.

**The decision is made twice, and that is the whole step.** It is tempting to read "sampling" as one
question with one answer. It is two questions, asked at two moments, with two different amounts of
information:

- **at the door** the only thing known is the trace id, so the decision is a *share* — and it has to
  be a function of the id rather than of the moment, because two hops that ask about one trace have
  to agree, and a counter or a clock makes them disagree. It is also the only moment at which the
  decision can be *recorded*: the deployment's share is a setting, and a setting that changes
  tomorrow must not rewrite what was decided today;
- **at the exit** the outcome is known, and that is exactly what the door could not see. **The trace
  an operator needs is the trace nobody asked for in advance** — a job that failed, spent its budget
  and became a dead letter. So a failing attempt is kept whatever the door said, and the worker is
  the only place where that can be decided.

What is *not* allowed is re-rolling. A hop that asks its own sampler again is deciding the same
question with less information than the door had and then throwing away the answer it was given — and
the trace it drops is the one it is inside. The door's answer travels on the row, the hop reads it,
and the exit's rule is `door said yes || this hop failed`: one function, in one place.

**A hop is a span, minted here.** The context has no span id in it, deliberately: the door and the
attempt are two hops, and a context carrying the door's span would make the whole queue look like one
step in the waterfall. Every attempt mints a span of its own, so a retry is visible as a retry rather
than as one long attempt — the same rule `relay/2.19` applied at a subscriber boundary, applied here
to a boundary that has a *queue* in it.

**What is missing is the trace, not the propagation.** There is no collector, no exporter and no
`otel` crate in this project, and adding one would not make the case this step is about any more
true: the decision is the value, the ids are on the row, and the fields are on the line. What a
deployment does with a kept trace is its own business, and `jobq/3.27-runbook-drill` is where an
operator gets to practise using it.

## TASK

Three places, and the scaffold has the first of them started:

### `src/engine/trace.rs` — the vocabulary

The scaffold added the module with the argument written down and six bodies missing: the two ids'
grammar, the header's, the sampler's decision, the door, and the exit. **Nothing declares it yet** —
the re-export in `src/engine/mod.rs` is the shape `jobq/3.16-dlq-tooling` used for `dlq` — and until
it is made the spec does not compile, which is the step telling you what it wants.

- `TraceId` / `SpanId` — the format's grammar, lower case, never all zeroes. An id this crate would
  not write is an id it does not continue;
- `Traceparent` — parse, `child`, `Display`: a header is parsed and regenerated, never forwarded;
- `Sampler` — the deployment's share, decided from the id: `always`, `never`, and a share in per
  mille. `decide` is the door's arithmetic;
- `Context` — what travels: `of` (the door), `stored` (the read half of the round trip),
  `exported` (the exit's rule), and the two accessors;
- `Hop` — one hop: the job's context and *this* attempt's span.

### The row — the carrier

`Job` gains the context, with `trace()` beside the other accessors, and `SubmitRequest` gains
`with_trace`: the caller that knows the ambient context builds it once and the engine stores what it
is given. Both stores keep it (`migrations/0007_traces.sql` — **the scaffold left it as an argument
rather than a statement**, and the three decisions it describes are yours), and `revived` carries it
through a replay without touching it.

A job nobody asked to trace has **no** trace rather than a false one: a `false` where the truth is
"nobody asked" is a series every dashboard then treats as a real decision.

### The line — the exit

`Attempt` gains the hop, and the worker mints one per attempt (`Hop::of(&context)`) and passes it in.
The line gains `trace_id`, `span_id` and `sampled`, written on every line and empty when the job has
no trace: the field set stays one shape, `sampled` is the *effective* decision rather than the door's
(a failed attempt shows `true` even when the door said no), and the whole thing is what makes
`grep trace_id=<the id your collector showed you>` a query.

### What deliberately does not change

Everything `jobq/3.1`–`jobq/3.17` certified: the `--check-config` keys, the `draining` line, the exit
code, the CLI's grammar and exit codes, the settle refusals, the claim order, the trail's identity,
the dead-letter view and its refusals, and the fields `jobq/3.17` put on a line. This step adds a
module, a field on the row, two columns and three fields on a line; nothing it already promised
moves.

## TRAP

1. **A context in a task-local or a span.** It works in the test that submits a job and watches the
   attempt happen right after it, and the attempt does not happen right after it: it happens in
   another process, minutes later, possibly after a restart. The row is the only carrier this queue
   has.
2. **Deciding at the exit only.** Sampling every failing job and nothing else sounds even better than
   the two-decision rule, and it is not available: the exit sees one attempt, not the trace — a
   success in the middle of a trace that fails later has already been dropped, and the waterfall an
   operator opens has a hole in the middle of it.
3. **Deciding at the door only.** The intuitive implementation, and it drops exactly the trace the
   step is named for: a job submitted with sampling off that fails three times leaves three warnings
   with no trace id anywhere, and the incident is a queue of ids nobody kept.
4. **Re-rolling at the hop.** `Sampler::decide` called again from the worker is the same mistake with
   a fresh coat of paint, and it is *worse* than deciding at the door only: it agrees with the door
   about half the traces by accident, so the traces that survive are the ones the coin toss kept and
   the ones the door kept are exactly the ones it drops.
5. **Regenerating the trace id at the worker.** "It is only an id" — and now the submitting side and
   the running side are two traces that share nothing but a timestamp, which is what the operator
   has instead of a waterfall.
6. **Reusing the parent's span.** One span for two hops makes the queue invisible: the attempt looks
   like part of the request that submitted it, and the four seconds the job waited are four seconds
   of the caller's latency.
7. **One span per job rather than per attempt.** A retry is a hop. A span shared between two attempts
   shows the second one as a continuation of the first, which is the opposite of what a retry is.
8. **Accepting the all-zero id, or upper case.** `0000…` is the format's own word for "there is no
   trace": accepting it puts every caller that sends it into one trace together, and upper case is
   the same id to a person and a different one to every index that stores it.
9. **Forwarding the caller's bytes.** A service that passes a header through becomes a relay for
   whatever anybody sends — including the ids that break in the next system. Parse it, keep the
   trace, generate the header you send.
10. **A trace that is not a column.** Putting the ids in the payload, or in a side table keyed by
    job, is a second place a job's identity lives — and the first one to go stale is the one nobody
    joined on.
