# A budget of zero is an alert, not a safety feature

## WHY

A PodDisruptionBudget is the promise a workload keeps to itself while the cluster does *voluntary*
work: node drains, upgrades, autoscaler consolidation. It has two spellings of one number —
`minAvailable` (at least this many stay up) and `maxUnavailable` (at most this many go down) — and
the arithmetic is the same in both directions.

The interesting cases are the edges:

- **a budget that allows zero evictions blocks every drain.** It is not a safety feature; it is a
  node that can never be drained, and an alert should fire on it long before somebody discovers it
  during an upgrade;
- **a budget larger than the workload cannot conjure pods.** `maxUnavailable: 9` on three replicas
  allows three, because that is all there is;
- **the floor cannot go below zero.** `minAvailable` bigger than the replica count is a
  misconfiguration; the answer is zero allowed, not a negative number.

## TASK

The scaffold added `src/budget.rs` and its `pub mod budget;` line.

- **[`allowed`]** — how many pods may be evicted at once: `replicas - minAvailable` for the first
  spelling, `maxUnavailable` for the second, capped at `replicas` and never below zero.

## DONE WHEN

- `kiln run platform/9.3-disruption-budget` — the spec passes
- `kiln check platform/9.3-disruption-budget` — every must gate is green
- `minAvailable: 2` of five allows three; `minAvailable: 5` of five allows none; `maxUnavailable: 9`
  of two allows two

## TRAP

1. **`minAvailable` and `maxUnavailable` at once.** Kubernetes rejects the manifest, and the model
   here has one budget: the API's answer is "pick a spelling", not "add them".
2. **A negative floor.** `replicas - minAvailable` on unsigned integers wraps: the arithmetic must
   saturate, or a misconfiguration becomes "evict everything".
3. **Treating zero as safe.** Zero allowed evictions means a drain that hangs; the number is a
   signal, and the step's job is to produce it correctly so the alert can act on it.
4. **Capping at the wrong thing.** The cap is the replica count, not the desired count after a
   scale-down: the budget is about pods that exist now.
