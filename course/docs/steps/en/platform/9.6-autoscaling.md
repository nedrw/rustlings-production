# Fast up, slow down, and a band that stops the flapping

## WHY

An autoscaler is one formula and two habits. The formula is
`desired = ceil(current × metric / target)`; the habits are what make it usable:

- **a tolerance band.** A metric inside 10% of the target is close enough. Without the band, a
  metric oscillating around the target asks for one replica more and then one less, every window,
  forever — the cluster spends its time launching and killing pods that never served anything;
- **asymmetry.** Scaling up happens on the first window, because the queue is growing *now*. Scaling
  down waits for several windows below target, because a short dip is a pause, and removing capacity
  for a pause is how the next spike meets a smaller service.

The asymmetry is the design decision worth defending in review. A symmetric autoscaler is easier to
describe and worse in both directions: slow to absorb a burst, and quick to throw away capacity.

## TASK

The scaffold added `src/autoscaling.rs` and its `pub mod autoscaling;` line.

- **[`desired`]** — the formula, clamped to `[min, max]`, with a metric inside
  `TOLERANCE_PERCENT` of the target leaving the current count alone.
- **[`Stabilizer::observe`]** — the count actually applied: a rise moves at once and resets the run;
  a fall waits for `down_after` consecutive windows at or below the current count.

## TRAP

1. **No band.** The formula alone oscillates around the target; the spec's *"inside the band leaves
   the count alone"* is the assertion that fails.
2. **Symmetric windows.** Waiting before scaling up is how a burst becomes an outage: the queue
   grows while the stabilizer counts.
3. **A float comparison for the band.** `metric as f64 / target as f64` decides a cluster's
   behaviour with a rounding rule; the integer form (`metric × 100` against `target × 90/110`) is
   exact.
4. **Clamping after the formula on the wrong side.** `max` is the ceiling and `min` the floor, and
   `min > max` is a configuration mistake the clamp must survive rather than panic on.
